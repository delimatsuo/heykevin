# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Solo contractors and trades workers: plumbers, electricians, HVAC techs, general contractors. Primary context is mid-job — on a roof, under a sink, elbow-deep in a panel — glancing at the screen with one eye and dirty hands to decide if an incoming call is a real lead or noise worth ignoring. They do not have patience for UI. Every second the app wastes is a second they're standing still on a job site.

Secondary: the personal mode targets busy professionals who want call noise filtered without missing people they actually care about.

## Product Purpose

Hey Kevin screens calls with an AI so contractors never miss a legitimate job inquiry while ignoring spam and robocalls. The app delivers a live transcript of what Kevin (the AI) is learning from the caller, so the contractor can make an instant decision: pick up, text back, or let Kevin take a message. Success means every real lead is captured, every robocall is silently handled, and the contractor never had to stop what they were doing to find out.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, fast, no-nonsense. Kevin is a reliable foreman in app form — never makes you wait, never decorates unnecessarily, gives you signal and gets out of the way. Tone is direct and practical. Confidence without attitude. The app does a serious job and should feel like it.

## References

**Admired**: Notion, Linear. Quiet confidence. Purposeful whitespace. Nothing decorative that doesn't earn its place. Information hierarchy that rewards a quick scan.

## Anti-references

- Any app that feels built for teenagers or consumer entertainment (rainbow gradients, emoji-heavy copy, bouncy animations, pastel playfulness)
- Glassmorphism, heavy shadows, or chrome used as decoration
- "Smart home" / concierge-app aesthetics — too premium-soft for a tradesperson audience
- Generic utility grey: the app should feel like Hey Kevin, not like every other iOS list app
- Excessive onboarding handholding: trades workers are competent, don't patronize them

## Design Principles

1. **Information first** — every pixel exists to help the contractor decide whether to pick up. Nothing decorative unless it cuts decision time.
2. **Outdoor readability** — screens are read in direct sunlight, at arm's length, through a smudged screen. Contrast and legibility always beat subtlety. When in doubt, go higher contrast.
3. **Speed is a value, not a metric** — the app must feel instant. UI latency is a product failure. Animations exist to orient, not to entertain; they must never make the user wait.
4. **Earned brand moments** — Hey Kevin has a distinct personality, expressed through precise color, weight, and voice — not through decoration and chrome.
5. **Respect the worker's hands** — tap targets, critical actions, and error recovery designed for gloves, sweat, and split-second attention. Never make the user aim precisely under pressure.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Extra care for outdoor use: elevated contrast ratios beyond AA floor, tap targets larger than Apple's 44pt minimum (aim 52pt+ for primary actions), text that holds up at arm's length in bright ambient light. Support Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, and reduce-motion. No critical information conveyed by color alone.
